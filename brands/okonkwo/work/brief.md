# Okonkwo-Marchetti Architectural Lighting Studio: brief

Okonkwo-Marchetti is an invented client, kept to test the generator and the renderer on a long name and a loud palette. The answers proposed here come from its Edition 1 fixture (`brands/okonkwo/brand.ts`); whoever answers plays the client. Edition 1's fonts (Bricolage Grotesque, Instrument Sans) are now rejected and are not carried over.

## Brief so far

1. Category: an independent architectural lighting design studio, designing the light in buildings and public spaces from concept to commissioning. Not a fixture manufacturer, not an electrical engineer, sells no products. (answered)
2. Three competitors: Speirs Major, Arup Lighting, L'Observatoire International. Speirs Major sets its name as plain capitals with a lowercase "light architecture" descriptor; Arup Lighting sits inside Arup's red corporate brand with full-width project photography; L'Observatoire International pairs a wordmark and symbol with "Lighting the World". Described from their sites' text only. (answered)

5. Never: spectacular (facades that change color every few seconds), technical (fixture catalogs and spec sheets), gloomy (black websites with glowing hero photography). (answered)
6. Budget tier: £40–70k, for an identity, a website system and stationery. (answered)
7. Deadline: March 2027, for a new website and a printed submission to a competition to relight a city bridge. (answered)
8. Mission and vision: we light buildings and streets for the people who use them after dark, with as much light as the place needs and no more; toward a city where the walk home at night is as good as the walk in daylight. (answered)

## Round 1

1. **[Category]** What does the studio do, in the words an architect would use, and what is it not?
   *Proposed:* an independent lighting design studio that designs the light in buildings and public spaces (lobbies, stairs, bridges, streets) from concept to commissioning. It is not a fixture manufacturer and not an electrical engineering firm, and it sells no products.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

2. **[Competitors]** Who does an architect also shortlist, and how does each one's identity look to you?
   *Proposed:* Speirs Major, Arup Lighting and L'Observatoire International. Because the studio is invented, their identities can stay undescribed; the scoring will then mark that criterion "not described" rather than guess.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

3. **[Audience]** Who signs, and who lives with the brand afterward?
   *Proposed:* architects and developers sign. Clients' marketing teams, planning committees and the public see the work afterward, mostly at night and in photographs.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

4. **[Equity]** Which parts of Edition 1 stay, change or go? And does the mark have to carry the full name, *Okonkwo-Marchetti Architectural Lighting Studio*, or can it be the surnames alone?
   - the two surnames on one line, joined by the hyphen
   - Sodium, the streetlight orange
   - Limestone and Basalt, the day and night pair
   - Filament yellow and Mercury teal
   - the voice traits Warm and Exacting
   *Proposed:* the mark is *Okonkwo-Marchetti*, with "Architectural Lighting Studio" as a descriptor set separately. Keep the one-line surnames and Sodium. Evolve the day and night pair. Drop Filament and Mercury unless a route earns them. Keep both voice traits.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

5. **[Never]** Three words the studio must never be, each with an example of a brand or a kind of work that is.
   *Proposed:* spectacular (facades that change color every few seconds), technical (fixture catalogs and spec sheets), gloomy (black websites with glowing hero photography).
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

6. **[Budget tier]** What would the studio pay for this identity?
   *Proposed:* £40–70k, a mid-size independent studio's fee for an identity, a website system and stationery.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

7. **[Deadline]** When, and for what?
   *Proposed:* March 2027: a new website and a printed submission for a competition to relight a city bridge.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

## Round 2

Added 2026-11-05, after the core grew to cover a complete brand book. Round 1's answers stand; this round asks only what the brand book needs and the brief lacks.

1. **[Mission and vision]** What does the studio do today, and what is it working toward?
   *Proposed:* Mission: we light buildings and streets for the people who use them after dark, with as much light as the place needs and no more. Vision: a city where the walk home at night is as good as the walk in daylight.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

2. **[Values]** Three or four values, each with what it means in the work.
   *Proposed:*
   - **Measured:** every lux level and color temperature has a reason we can say out loud.
   - **Darkness is designed:** we decide where light doesn't go as carefully as where it does.
   - **Warm first:** warm white and sodium tones before anything cooler, and never color for its own sake.
   - **There at switch-on:** we commission what we design, on site, the night it's turned on.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

3. **[Competitors]** How does each competitor's identity look? Their sites could be read as text but not seen, so this proposal holds only what the text shows; correct or add what you know of how they look.
   *Proposed:*
   - **Speirs Major:** the name set as plain type in capitals with "light architecture" as a lowercase descriptor; a project-led, sparse site. (Text only; colors and imagery not seen.)
   - **Arup Lighting:** a service line inside Arup's corporate brand, with Arup's red logo; full-width project photography, including night scenes, and card grids. The identity is Arup's, not the lighting team's.
   - **L'Observatoire International:** a wordmark with a symbol, the line "Lighting the World", and offices listed by city (New York, Paris, Seoul, London). (Text only; colors and imagery not seen.)
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

4. **[Applications]** Which pieces must the brand book show working?
   *Proposed:* from the deadline's deliverables: the website (home page), the printed competition board for the bridge (A1, portrait), business card and letterhead, email signature. From the category: a site hoarding around a project under construction, a social post announcing a finished project, and a credentials deck cover for architects.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

5. **[Voice]** Two more lines (write this, not that) and one more rewrite, so the voice has examples beyond the stair.
   *Proposed:*
   - Write "The bridge is lit from the handrail, so the river stays dark and the stars stay out." Not "A stunning illuminated landmark for the city."
   - Write "We'll be on site the night it's switched on." Not "Full project lifecycle support."
   - Rewrite: before "Our award-winning team delivers bespoke lighting experiences." After "Six of us, all lighting designers. We've lit 40 buildings and 3 bridges."
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.

6. **[Logo]** Rules for the marks beyond the general misuse examples.
   *Proposed:*
   - Never replace the beam with a typed hyphen.
   - Never set "Architectural Lighting Studio" on the wordmark's line; it sits below or beside, in the label style.
   - Never put the marks in Stone on Sodium (2.45:1).
   - Never set the marks over a photograph's light; place them on a Night or Sodium field cut into the image.
   **Answer:** Accepted as proposed.
